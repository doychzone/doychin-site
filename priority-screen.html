The Four Horsemen

Don't die from something stupid.

There is a running joke in longevity circles that the single best tactic is exactly that: don't die from something stupid. It gets a laugh, and then it lands, because that is most of the game.

Longevity, underneath the noise, is the long and unglamorous work of not being taken out early by the things most likely to do it. Keep avoiding the big ones for long enough and time stays on your side. And the big ones are surprisingly few. Four areas cause most of what shortens life, and they are sometimes called the Four Horsemen.

Cardiovascular disease

The world's leading killer, and one of the most preventable. It builds silently for years, which is exactly why it rewards looking early.

Metabolic dysfunction

How your body handles energy and blood sugar. The quiet upstream driver that makes the other three more likely.

Cancer

The one where timing decides everything. Found early the odds change completely, so screening and family history matter most.

Neurodegenerative disease

The slow one. Its groundwork is laid decades before symptoms, which is why early habits matter most here.

They do not appear overnight. Long before a diagnosis, there are signals hidden in family history, everyday measurements, habits, symptoms, and screening history.

The problem is rarely a lack of information. It is knowing what matters, what is missing, and where to begin.

So What?

One question. Two directions.

Tuesday, September 29, 2026About a 2 minute read

A friend and I have a pretty stupid game.

We sit down for a beer, and one of us makes a statement. Anything.

"I got 12,000 steps today."

The other has one job. He replies:

"So what?"

So you try to explain why it matters.

"So what?"

You explain deeper.

"So what?"

And it keeps going, one "so what?" at a time, until you get tangled, or can no longer say why the thing actually matters, or simply say: "I don't know." Then you drink.

A stupid game.

But lately I think maybe it is not so stupid.

Because a lot of what we hear every day does not survive even two or three "so what?"s in a row.

A new study.
So what?
A new technology.
So what?
Some famous person does something.
So what?
Someone found a new way to live longer, train better, sleep deeper, be more productive.
So what?

I am not saying these things do not matter.

The opposite.

The question is whether we know what they mean. For us. Now. And whether anything actually worth doing follows from them.

It is very easy to be impressed by information. It is much harder to run it through a few simple questions:

Why does this matter?
Where am I in this?
And what can I do from here?

Maybe that is one of the reasons I started Making Sense.

Not because we lack information. We have too much.

We lack a filter.

And sometimes the best filter is one annoying question.

So what?

This was the whole idea behind Issue Zero: what deserves my attention? "So what?" is that same question, stripped down to two words you can use over a beer.

One question. Two directions.

It can close the conversation.

"So what?"
I don't care.
It does not matter.
Next.

But it can also open it.

"So what?"
Why is this important?
What does it change?
What am I still missing?
What should I do?

The same question.

Two completely different directions.

And maybe that is exactly where the test is.

Not in the "so what?" itself.

But in what we do after it.

If the question makes us think more deeply, it is worth it. If we use it only to dismiss something, we have probably stopped too early.

TEMELI: Mindfulness
Not sitting on a cushion. Just catching, in the moment, whether you are opening or closing.

And by the way, if you got this far and still do not know why this matters...

By the rules of my game, you owe me a beer.

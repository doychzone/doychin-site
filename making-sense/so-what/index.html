So What?

One question. Two directions.

Wednesday, September 30, 2026About a 3 minute read

Good morning, Sensemakers!

Today, a silly drinking game, Morpheus’s choice, and why you might owe me a beer by the end.

But first...

The photo above was taken with my friend Iliyan.

It also captures the perfect setting for one of our favorite silly games.

Two friends. A beer. And one very annoying question.

One of us makes a statement. Anything.

“I got 12,000 steps today.”

The other has one job.

He replies:

“So what?”

So you try to explain why it matters.

“So what?”

You explain a little deeper.

“So what?”

And it keeps going, one “so what?” at a time, until you get tangled, can no longer explain why the thing actually matters, or simply say:

“I don’t know.”

Then you drink.

A silly game.

But lately, I have started to think that maybe it is not so silly.

A lot of what we hear every day does not survive even two or three “so what?”s in a row.

A new study.
So what?
A new technology.
So what?
Some famous person does something.
So what?
Someone discovers a new way to live longer, train better, sleep deeper or become more productive.
So what?

I am not saying these things do not matter.

The opposite.

The question is whether we understand what they mean.

For us. Now.

And whether anything worth doing actually follows from them.

It is easy to be impressed by information.

It is much harder to run it through a few simple questions:

Why does this matter?
Where am I in this?
What can I do from here?

Maybe that is one of the reasons I started Making Sense.

Not because we lack information.

We have too much.

What we lack is a filter.

And sometimes the best filter is one annoying question.

So what?

This was also the idea behind Issue Zero: what truly deserves my attention?

“So what?” is that same question, stripped down to two words you can use over a beer.

One question. Two directions.

There is a scene in The Matrix where Morpheus offers Neo two pills.

Take one, and the story ends. You return to what is familiar.

Take the other, and you continue, without knowing exactly what you will discover.

There is no universally correct choice.

Most of us have probably chosen both at different moments.

Our choice may depend on our experience, confidence, curiosity, fatigue or even how well we slept the night before.

“So what?” can work in much the same way.

The question itself is neutral.

What matters is the direction we take after asking it.

One version returns us to what we already know.

“So what?”
I have heard this before.
It does not matter.
Next.

The other keeps us in the conversation.

“So what?”
Why is this important?
What does it change?
What am I still missing?
What should I do?

The same question.

Two completely different directions.

And maybe that is exactly where the test is.

Not in the words “so what?”

But in what we do after them.

Do they open a door?

Or do they close one?

If the question helps us think more deeply, it is useful.

If we use it only to dismiss something, we may have stopped too early.

TEMELI: Mindfulness
Not sitting on a cushion. Just noticing, in the moment, whether your question is opening the conversation or closing it.

For the next few hours, try to catch yourself asking:

“So what?”

Do not examine only the information in front of you.

Examine your response to it.

Which pill did you just choose?

And by the way, if you reached the end and still do not know why any of this matters...

By the rules of our game, you owe me a beer.

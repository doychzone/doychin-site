DAILY STRETCH & GOLF WARM-UP

Built for Christopher Kohnen

Your everyday plan for a happier back and knee, and a better golf game.

Why this approach

Chris, you have a back spasm and some fluid in the knee, no pain right now, and you want to play golf this week. So the rotation comes from your upper back and hips while your lower back stays quiet, your knee is never deeply bent or kneeled on, and we keep it strong with low-load work, all pain free. The golf tournament this weekend got us started, but these are stretches you need every day, which is why I took the time to turn them into this little app made just for you, to make them more fun. If anything does not feel right, skip it and tell me. Enjoy the round. Doych

The rules

1

Pain-free only

A mild stretch is fine. Pain means stop or ease off.

2

Protect the knee

No kneeling, no deep bending, no sharp twisting of the knee.

3

Slow, with breath

No bouncing at end range. Breathe calmly.

4

Two routines

Daily Stretch: every morning at home, 12 to 15 min. Before Play: 8 to 10 min standing, with a club.

This is a warm-up, not treatment. If in doubt, see a doctor.

DAILY STRETCH 12 to 15 min

Chris's Daily Stretch

Every morning at home. Strap, band and yoga block.
The tournament got us started, but this one is for every day, not just golf week.

1

Knee-to-chest

3 x 20 sper side

Lie on your back, strap behind the thigh (not on the knee), gently draw the knee toward your chest; other leg bent, foot flat.

Why for golf: Relaxes the lower back and glutes, which absorb rotation in the golf swing.

Caution: Never pull into pain. If the knee complains, keep it less bent.

2

Open book

8per side

Lie on your side, block under the head and between the knees, knees bent to 90 degrees; open the top arm back and follow it with your eyes.

Why for golf: Rotation comes from the upper back, not the lower back. That is the base of a good swing.

Caution: Open only as far as is comfortable; pelvis and knees stay together.

3

Strap hamstring stretch

2 to 3 x 30 sper side

Lie on your back, strap around the foot, lift the leg with a soft knee; other leg bent.

Why for golf: Tight hamstrings pull on the pelvis and load the lower back in the address position.

Caution: Keep the knee slightly bent. No pulling behind the knee.

4

Supine figure-4

2 x 30 sper side

On your back, ankle crossed over the opposite thigh, strap behind the lower thigh, draw gently toward you.

Why for golf: Frees the hip so the hip rotates, not the lower back.

Caution: If the knee dislikes it, keep the foot on the floor and just let the knee fall out.

5

Glute bridge with block

2 x 10slow

On your back, knees bent, block squeezed between the knees; squeeze the glutes and lift the hips to a straight line.

Why for golf: Strong glutes protect the lower back and stabilize the swing.

Caution: No arching in the lower back. Easier: lift the hips only halfway.

6

Quad sets and straight leg raise

10 x 5 s + 10raises

Tighten the thigh of the straight leg for 5 s, then lift the straight leg to the height of the bent knee.

Why for golf: Wakes up the quadriceps, which switches off with a swollen knee, and protects the joint.

Caution: No bending the knee under load. Lower slowly.

7

Standing cat-camel

8slow reps

Stand, knees soft, hands on thighs. Round the back up, then gently straighten it. Breathe.

Why for golf: Gentle mobility for the whole spine with no lying down or kneeling.

Caution: Small range in the lower back. Stop if you feel a pull down the leg.

8

Hip flexor stretch at the wall

2 x 30 sper side

Hands on the wall, one foot back, squeeze the glute and tuck the pelvis until you feel a stretch at the front of the hip.

Why for golf: A relaxed hip flexor reduces load on the lower back as you turn.

Caution: No kneeling. Front knee only slightly bent.

9

Band pull-apart

12 to 15reps

Band in front of the chest, arms straight; pull apart to the sides and squeeze the shoulder blades.

Why for golf: Straightens the upper back and prepares the shoulders for the swing.

Caution: Lighter band or wider grip if the shoulders tense up.

BEFORE PLAY 8 to 10 min

Chris's Before Play

On the course, standing, with a club

1

Brisk walk

1 to 2 min

Walk briskly with a natural arm swing.

Why for golf: Raises muscle temperature and heart rate before you swing.

Caution: Flat ground, no jumping.

2

Standing pelvic tilts

10slow

Stand, hands on hips, knees soft. Tuck the pelvis under, then gently tip it back.

Why for golf: Wakes up the lower back without loading it.

Caution: Small range, only in the pain-free zone.

3

Club thoracic rotation

8 to 10per side

Club behind the shoulders, golf stance; turn the chest to the side while the pelvis stays almost still.

Why for golf: Rehearses the exact swing rotation, but from the upper back.

Caution: No bouncing at the end of the movement.

4

Leg swings

8 to 10forward and sideways

Lean on the club, swing the leg forward and back, then sideways across the body.

Why for golf: Opens the hip for turning and weight transfer.

Caution: Small range at first. Standing leg with a soft knee.

5

Mini band side steps

10each way

Band above the knees, shallow quarter squat, step sideways with a tall back.

Why for golf: Activates the glutes, which stabilize knee and pelvis in the swing.

Caution: Very shallow squat. Skip the band if the knee gets irritated.

6

Hip hinge

8 to 10reps

Club along the back (head, shoulder blades, tailbone); push the hips back with a flat back, knees slightly bent.

Why for golf: Teaches the golf posture from the hips, not the lower back.

Caution: Knees to about 45 degrees, no deep bending.

7

Band pull-apart

10reps

Straight arms in front of the chest; pull the band apart, shoulder blades together.

Why for golf: Final activation of the upper back and shoulders.

Caution: Lighter band if the shoulder feels strained.

8

Progressive practice swings

5 + 5 + 350%, 75%, optional 90%

5 swings at 50%, 5 at 75%, optionally 3 at 90%, smooth and balanced.

Why for golf: Golf-specific warm-up has the clearest effect on performance and safety.

Caution: Stop with sharp pain. Stay at 75% if the back feels tight.

After Play, 5 minutes

  • Slow walk, 1 to 2 min.
  • Club rotation, 5 per side.
  • Hamstring stretch with a strap or with the heel on a bench, 30 s.
  • Standing hip flexor stretch, 30 s per side.
  • At home: knee-to-chest, 30 s per side.
  • If the knee is swollen: ice for 10 to 15 min, through a towel.

Red Flags: when to stop

  • Pain radiating down the leg, numbness or weakness.
  • Bladder or bowel trouble, or numbness in the groin: this is an emergency, get medical help now.
  • Knee locking, giving way or swelling more.
  • Sharp pain during a swing.

Tips for this week

  • Use a cart for at least the first 9 holes.
  • Play 9 holes at about 80%, with fewer full driver swings.
  • On the range: half swings with a wedge, then mid irons, driver last. 20 to 30 balls in total.
  • Pick the ball up by squatting on the good leg or with a ball pickup. No bending from the lower back.
